ANTI STOIC
IMAGE DIRECTION GUIDE

This document defines the visual language for every image created for the AntiStoic brand.

Every image should immediately feel like it belongs to the same world.

The visual identity is timeless, restrained, editorial, architectural, and emotionally powerful.

--------------------------------------------------

CORE IDEA

The artwork should feel like discovering forgotten pieces inside an ancient museum.

Nothing should feel modern.

Nothing should feel trendy.

Nothing should feel AI generated.

The artwork should feel like it already existed centuries ago.

--------------------------------------------------

EMOTIONAL GOALS

Every image should evoke:

Stillness

Discipline

Mortality

Perspective

Responsibility

Quiet confidence

Reflection

Mystery

Timelessness

The viewer should pause.

Not simply look.

--------------------------------------------------

STYLE

Luxury Editorial

Museum Exhibition

Neo-Classical

Architectural

High Fashion

Fine Art

19th Century Copperplate Engraving

Marble Sculpture

Museum Photography

Editorial Minimalism

Never fantasy.

Never gaming.

Never comic book.

Never cinematic action.

--------------------------------------------------

COLOR PALETTE

Pure Black background.

Pure White subject whenever possible.

Extremely high contrast.

Very subtle gray only for depth.

No colors.

No gold.

No blue.

No red.

No neon.

No gradients.

Monochrome only.

--------------------------------------------------

LIGHTING

Museum lighting.

Directional.

Soft but dramatic.

Deep shadows.

Strong highlights.

Large negative space.

The subject should emerge from darkness.

Never flat lighting.

--------------------------------------------------

TEXTURE

Weathered marble.

Stone.

Ancient paper.

Copperplate engraving.

Fine etched linework.

Subtle film grain.

Light dust.

Very restrained.

Never overprocessed.

--------------------------------------------------

SUBJECTS

Prefer:

Roman busts

Greek statues

Broken marble sculptures

Ancient architecture

Columns

Column capitals

Stone staircases

Stone arches

Ancient doors

Sculpted hands

Ancient books

Stone tablets

Museum artifacts

Architectural fragments

Winged marble figures

Atlas

Nike

Classical allegorical figures

Hands reaching

Broken laurel wreaths

Avoid generic Marcus Aurelius busts unless specifically requested.

--------------------------------------------------

COMPOSITION

Large negative space.

Asymmetrical balance.

Subjects partially cropped.

Images may bleed off the canvas.

Subjects should not always be centered.

The artwork should leave room for typography.

Editorial composition over symmetrical composition.

--------------------------------------------------

MOOD

Calm.

Quiet.

Heavy.

Elegant.

Expensive.

Timeless.

Architectural.

Reflective.

Never aggressive.

Never loud.

Never busy.

--------------------------------------------------

CAMERA

If using photography:

Museum quality.

Medium format feel.

Fine art photography.

Extremely sharp.

Large depth.

No wide-angle distortion.

No HDR.

--------------------------------------------------

GRAPHIC DESIGN

No icons.

No floating UI.

No fake overlays.

No random particles.

No unnecessary textures.

Typography should remain separate from artwork unless specifically requested.

--------------------------------------------------

WHAT TO AVOID

Do NOT create:

Generic motivational posters

Spartan helmets

Lions

Wolves

Fire

Lightning

War scenes

Blood

Explosions

Muscular fantasy warriors

Roman soldiers yelling

Overly detailed fantasy armor

Crypto aesthetics

AI glow

Cyberpunk

Synthwave

Gold trim

Inspirational social media graphics

Anything resembling a quote account.

--------------------------------------------------

REFERENCE BRANDS

Apple

Aesop

COS

Saint Laurent

The Row

Kinfolk

The Getty Museum

The Metropolitan Museum of Art

Luxury editorial magazines

Museum exhibition catalogs

--------------------------------------------------

QUALITY TEST

Before generating an image ask:

Could this hang inside a modern art museum?

Would this work as a luxury fashion campaign?

Would someone mistake this for a stock image?

If yes...

Start over.
